# Spectrum of a finite-step Kuramoto graphon

## Setup

Partition `[0,1]` into cells `I_1,...,I_k` of positive masses `p_i`. Let the
symmetric graphon be constant on each rectangle:

```text
W(x,y) = w_ij  for x in I_i, y in I_j.
```

Let the equilibrium phase also be constant on cells, with value `phi_i` on
`I_i`. Define

```text
c_ij = w_ij cos(phi_i-phi_j),
q_i  = sum_j p_j c_ij.
```

The Hessian operator is

```text
(H v)(x) = integral W(x,y) cos(phi(x)-phi(y)) [v(x)-v(y)] dy.
```

The flow linearization is `L=-H`.

## Theorem — exact invariant splitting

Let `S` be the `k`-dimensional space of functions constant on every cell, and
let

```text
Z = {v : integral over I_i of v = 0 for every i}.
```

Then

```text
L2([0,1]) = S orthogonal-direct-sum Z,
```

and both spaces are invariant under `H`.

On `S`, using mass-orthonormal coordinates `r_i=sqrt(p_i) v_i`, `H` is the
symmetric matrix

```text
Q_ii = sum_{j != i} p_j c_ij,
Q_ij = -sqrt(p_i p_j)c_ij,  i != j.
```

On `Z`, for `x in I_i`,

```text
(H v)(x) = q_i v(x).
```

Therefore the full Hessian spectrum is the union of the quotient spectrum and
the cellwise values `q_i` (with the appropriate infinite multiplicities for
non-atomic cells).

## Proof

Cell averaging is the orthogonal projection onto `S`; its kernel is exactly
`Z`, giving the direct-sum decomposition.

For `v` constant on cells, substitute `v(y)=v_j` into the integral:

```text
(H v)_i = sum_j p_j c_ij(v_i-v_j).
```

The `j=i` term vanishes. Changing variables to `r_i=sqrt(p_i)v_i` gives the
displayed symmetric quotient matrix. Its rotation vector is
`(sqrt(p_1),...,sqrt(p_k))`.

Now let `v` lie in `Z`. Since `c_ij` is constant on every rectangle,

```text
integral over I_j of c_ij v(y) dy
  = c_ij integral over I_j of v(y) dy
  = 0.
```

Thus the integral term involving `v(y)` vanishes on every cell, while the
coefficient of `v(x)` is exactly `q_i`. Hence `H v=q_i v` on `I_i`.

## Stability criterion

The step equilibrium is strictly linearly stable modulo rotation exactly when:

1. `Q` has one zero eigenvalue generated by rotation and all other eigenvalues
   are positive; and
2. every `q_i` is positive.

This theorem explains why quotient stability alone is insufficient and why
the transverse quantities in the finite witness audits have a graphon
analogue.
